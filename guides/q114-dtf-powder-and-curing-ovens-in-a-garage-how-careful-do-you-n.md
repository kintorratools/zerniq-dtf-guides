## 7. DTF Powder and Curing Oven Fire Safety in a Garage

A garage can be a higher-risk DTF location because it often mixes heat, dust, electricity, and flammable storage.

Key risks include:

- **Powder dust:** Treat DTF powder as a potential combustible dust unless your SDS says otherwise.
- **Heat source:** A curing oven is designed to generate heat. Keep it away from paper, film, solvents, cardboard, and fuel.
- **Vapors:** Gasoline, propane, paint thinner, and lawn equipment fuel can create flammable vapors.
- **Electrical load:** Heaters and ovens draw power. Avoid overloaded circuits, damaged cords, and daisy-chained extension leads.
- **Poor ventilation:** Garages may trap fumes, heat, and fine dust.
- **Unattended operation:** Many DTF fire risks become worse when nobody is watching.

Minimum safety habits:

- Read the DTF powder SDS and curing oven manual before first use.
- Keep powder sealed, labeled, and cleaned up immediately.
- Do not cure near gasoline, propane, solvents, or paint.
- Do not use a curing oven for food.
- Keep clearance around the oven as required by its manual.
- Use ventilation appropriate to the space and materials.
- Keep a fire extinguisher suitable for your local code nearby.
- Stop operation if you smell burning, see smoke, or notice tripped breakers.
- Confirm your insurance covers the setup.not published

If you cannot provide ventilation, separation, electrical safety, and active fire controls, do not set up a curing oven in a garage. The safer choice is a dedicated, code-compliant workspace.

Be very careful. DTF powder can create dust, and curing ovens create heat. You need SDS review, ventilation, powder cleanup, electrical safety, fire separation, and a no-unattended rule. This is not a casual craft setup if you are using a garage.

### Is there a fire hazard if I set it up in my garage?

Yes, there is a potential fire hazard. The risk increases if the garage stores fuel, solvents, propane, paint, or clutter. It also increases with powder buildup, overheated equipment, damaged cords, poor ventilation, and unattended curing. A garage setup can reduce risk only with active controls.
