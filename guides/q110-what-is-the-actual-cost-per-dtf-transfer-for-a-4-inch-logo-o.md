## What “Actual Cost per Transfer” Means

“Actual cost per transfer” is not the same as “sale price.” It is the fully loaded production cost of one finished transfer before profit.

Use this formula:

**Cost per transfer = film + ink + powder + electricity + labor + depreciation + overhead + waste/reprints**

Each part must be measured in real units:

- **Film:** price per m² × transfer area in m².
- **Ink:** price per mL × measured mL per transfer.
- **Powder:** price per gram × measured grams per transfer.
- **Electricity:** equipment watts ÷ 1000 × hours × kWh rate ÷ transfers per batch.
- **Labor:** hourly rate × minutes per transfer ÷ 60.
- **Depreciation:** printer cost ÷ expected lifetime transfers.
- **Overhead:** rent, software, maintenance, shipping, and taxes ÷ monthly transfers.
- **Waste/reprints:** failed transfer rate × cost per attempt.

For a 4-inch logo, start with the bounding box. A 4-inch square logo is **16 in²**, which equals **103.2 cm²**, or **0.01032 m²**. If the logo is not square, measure the actual bounding box. Do not price a 4-inch-wide irregular logo as a full square unless your film and ink usage justify it.

## What Changes the Cost Most

- **Logo size and shape:** a 4-inch square uses more film and ink than a 4-inch-wide thin logo.
- **White ink laydown:** dark work shirts usually need white ink, which can raise ink cost.
- **Film nesting:** placing logos closer together can reduce film waste.
- **Powder control:** over-powdering wastes material and can hurt hand feel.
- **Electricity rate:** curing and heat pressing can dominate energy use.
- **Labor speed:** manual powder and press steps often cost more than ink.
- **Reprint rate:** every failed transfer doubles part of the cost.
- **Depreciation volume:** higher monthly volume lowers depreciation per transfer.
- **Maintenance:** clogged XP600 printheads can increase waste and downtime.
- **Local pricing:** film, ink, powder, and electricity are location-dependent.
